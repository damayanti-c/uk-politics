# Audit report: Reform council spending (interim intent study)
2026-10-30. Log: `review/audit-log.md`.

## 1. Verdict

The headline conclusion holds: Reform's first budgets show no large reallocation, few genuinely new
discretionary cuts, no named EDI cuts, and a real but uneven cut to the council tax rise. Every
script re-runs and reproduces the committed outputs exactly.

It is not ready to publish as written. One code bug contaminates the control group. Two factual
errors appear in the text. The "no different from non-Reform councils" claim is stronger than a
test with 11 councils can support. The savings coding treats similar technical lines
differently across councils. A combined-authority transport transfer is a confound that the
write-up does not mention.

## 5. Replication

All six scripts were run in order. `git status` was clean afterwards, so every CSV in `data/` and

tax DiD all match the write-ups.

## 6. Red team (0 kill shots, 5 major, 4 moderate)

**R1 (major): the combined-authority transport transfer.** EMCCA took over buses and concessionary
fares from Derbyshire and Nottinghamshire on 1 Feb 2026, and Lancashire CCA became the transport
authority from 1 Apr 2026. Highways & transport budgets fell **-45% (Derbyshire), -48.5%
(Nottinghamshire) and -12% (Lancashire)**. These three councils drive the "treated -15pp highways
DiD", the "treated squeezed discretionary 0.34pp more" result, and both negative Filter 4 rows.
This is most likely a function transfer, not a Reform choice. Fix: exclude transport or add back
the CCA levy, then re-run 03 and Filter 4.

**R2 (major): "no different from controls" is absence of evidence.** Welch p=0.74 and
Mann-Whitney p=0.66. But the minimum detectable difference at 80% power is **0.6pp of budget**
against a control mean of 0.34%. The test could not detect Reform cutting nearly three times as
much as controls. Suggested wording: "similar in scale; the sample is too small to rule out
moderate differences."

**R3 (major): technical lines are coded differently across councils.** Gloucestershire "Capital
reprofiling" is coded `cost_avoidance / no` and excluded. Hertfordshire "Capital reprogramming
one-off" (£2.1m), "Capitalisation savings (in-year)" (£2.2m) and three capitalise-staff lines
(£0.9m) are coded `cashable / partial` and counted as genuine cuts. On the treated side,
Derbyshire's "Corporate Risk Management Budget - brought-forward balance offsets payroll pressure"
(£5.4m) is counted as a cut. With one consistent rule, Herts falls from ~0.96% to ~0.74% and
Derbyshire from 0.86% to ~0.49%. The headline treated discretionary figure moves from £43.5m to
about £38m. "Largest cutter is a Lib Dem council" survives, but by a margin that depends on
these coding calls.

**R4 (major): Worcestershire is left out.** The "eight of nine shire counties that cut their rise
are Reform-led" line includes the minority counties Leicestershire (-2.0) and Warwickshire
(-0.56). The write-up never mentions that Reform-led Worcestershire raised by **8.98%** (+4.0pp),
so the minority group's mean change is **+0.48pp**. A hostile reader will call this selective.
Also, the ninth "cutter" (Hampshire, -0.01) is a rounding artefact.

**R5 (major): the controls also changed hands.** All five flipped to Lib Dem or Lib Dem-led NOC in
2025. The DiD therefore estimates "Reform vs a Lib Dem change of administration", not
"Reform vs no change". The write-up says this once. The briefing contradicts it (see section 4).

**R6 (moderate): ceiling effect.** The controls sat at the 4.99% referendum cap, so they could
only stay put or fall. The DiD is still informative, but say this.

**R7 (moderate): the keyword scan only finds lines named as EDI.** EDI and climate spending
usually sits inside staffing and corporate lines, so "0 EDI lines" is weak evidence of "0 EDI
cuts". The scan also misses "inclusion", "carbon" and "sustainab". Running those terms adds
almost nothing (£0.04m treated), so the conclusion holds, but phrase it as "no *named* EDI
savings".

**R8 (moderate): £27m of stated savings is not coded.** Kent is missing £21m and Lancashire £19m;
Lincolnshire and Nottinghamshire are over-coded. Reconcile, or show stated vs coded per council
in the main text.

**R9 (moderate): Kent's reversals are coded as `carried_forward`.** The -£18.0m of
"removal of undeliverable saving" lines reduces the inherited deduction, so Kent's "new savings"
(£58.7m) exceed its extracted headline (£40.7m). Gross inherited across the six is £61.6m, not
£48.5m.

## 7. Falsifiability and tripwires

**Placebo (FAIL, below the stop threshold).** Discretionary-share DiD, treated minus the 5 real
controls:

| Window | DiD (pp) |
|---|---|
| 2023-24 to 2024-25 (pre) | +0.76 |
| 2024-25 to 2025-26 (pre) | -0.86 |
| 2025-26 to 2026-27 (Reform) | -0.41 |

The Reform-year figure is inside the pre-period noise. This supports the "no compositional shift"
headline, but it means the "treated squeezed 0.34pp more" line should not be presented as a real
effect.

**Testable now, not yet run:**
- A council tax placebo on 2024/25 to 2025/26 (needs the 2023/24 Band D column).
- Re-run 03 with transport excluded (R1).

**Tripwires:**

| Date | Data | Claim it tests | Escalate if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feb-Mar 2027 | 2027/28 council tax | Reform tax-rise cut persists | Reform-majority mean change vs controls is > -0.2pp (effect gone) |
| ~Jun 2027 | 2027/28 RA budgets | No compositional shift | Treated discretionary-share DiD beyond ±0.9pp (outside placebo range) |
| ~Sep-Dec 2027 | 2026/27 outturn | Discretionary cuts £30-44m and delivered | Outturn discretionary change in treated falls by more than 2x the coded cut, or rises vs budget |
| Ongoing | £500 transparency data | No hidden EDI cuts | Any EDI/consultancy supplier line cut ≥ £0.5m not in the savings schedules |
